Lesson 1 of 4Introduction to AI in the Cloud

1. Why AI Infrastructure Is Hard

Building and running solid AI infrastructure now decides who succeeds in the AI era. The transcript names three challenges:

Challenge Why it hurts
Design complexity Sophisticated models need more powerful hardware and specialized software, which can cause unpredictable performance and scaling problems
Deployment time You have to procure, install and integrate hardware, software and networking. That means delays and higher costs
Training and ramping to production It takes lots of data, compute and expertise to train accurate models. Because models keep being updated, ramping production to meet business demand is hard

2. What "AI in the Cloud" Means

  • AI in the cloud is the integration of AI technologies with cloud computing infrastructure. It gives businesses scalable compute and ready-made ML services.

Five benefits

Benefit What it gives you
Computational power Access to huge compute without buying lots of on-premises hardware
Scalability and flexibility Adjust resources dynamically with demand, so capacity is used efficiently
Accessible ML services Pre-built AI services such as speech recognition and NLP that developers can plug in
Data management and integration Tools to manage, store and process data for better insights and decisions
Cost-effectiveness and collaboration No upfront investment, pay as you go. Teams share models and datasets in real time

3. How AI and Cloud Computing Reinforce Each Other

Area What the combination delivers
High-performance computing ML needs powerful CPUs and GPUs. Public, private or hybrid clouds supply them, chosen by security and compliance needs
Automation Automates data analysis, management, security and decision-making, which raises efficiency and cuts cost
Advanced cloud services Serverless computing, batch processing and container orchestration for integrating and scaling ML apps
Data-driven insights ML gives impartial interpretation of large structured, unstructured or raw datasets
Global accessibility AI services reachable from anywhere with an internet connection
Accelerated innovation Rapid development, testing and deployment of models encourages experimentation

Key Terms

  • AI in the cloud: AI technologies running on cloud computing infrastructure, giving scalable compute and ML services.
  • Public / private / hybrid cloud: the cloud types that can supply AI compute, chosen by security and compliance needs.
  • Pay as you go: paying only for the cloud resources you use, with no upfront investment.
  • Serverless computing: running code without managing servers, one of the cloud services that support ML.

Exam Tips

  • The three infrastructure challenges are design complexity, deployment time, and training and ramping to production.
  • The five cloud benefits are compute power, scalability, accessible ML services, data management, and cost and collaboration.
  • The cloud removes upfront investment (pay as you go) and gives access to pre-built services like speech recognition and NLP.
  • Cloud services that support ML: serverless, batch processing, container orchestration.

Lesson 2 of 4AI Use Cases in the Cloud

1. Managing AI Workloads and Infrastructure in the Cloud

Good workload and infrastructure management is critical for efficiency, scalability and performance in the cloud.

Activity What it covers
Cloud-based model deployment management Deploy ML models at scale, automate deployment, and manage model versions and updates
Cloud-native management and orchestration Cloud-native tools: containerization, service meshes, serverless computing, for scalability, agility and high availability
Cloud-based cluster management Deploy, manage and scale containerized apps across distributed cloud environments
Infrastructure acceleration libraries Pre-built components (for containers and serverless) for fast deployment. They improve utilization, cut latency and increase scalability

2. The AI Development Workflow in the Cloud

  • Developing and training AI is notoriously resource-intensive, which makes the cloud an ideal place for AI development.
Phase What happens
Data preparation Turn raw data into a form that suits ML models
Model training Teach the model to interpret and learn from data so it performs a task accurately
Simulate and test Iteratively improve accuracy and reduce error
Deployment Make the model available to other systems so they can send data and get predictions back
  • The same tools from Unit 5 (AI Software Ecosystem) work in the cloud.

3. Common AI Use Cases for the Cloud

Chosen for the cloud because of its scalability, flexibility and cost-effectiveness:

Use case What it does
LLMs Accelerate NLP, automate communication, improve customer interactions
Speech AI Chatbots and virtual assistants for customer support. Speech recognition to transcribe audio and video
Recommendation engines Personalize customer experiences
Fraud detection and prevention Spot and stop fraud in real time
Sentiment analysis Use NLP and ML to measure emotional tone and opinions in text
Supply chain optimization Demand forecasting, inventory management, route planning
Predictive maintenance Predict equipment failures to prevent unplanned downtime

Key Terms

  • Service mesh: a cloud-native layer that manages communication between services.
  • Model version management: tracking and updating deployed model versions.
  • Large language model (LLM): a model for NLP tasks, a top cloud use case.
  • Speech AI: chatbots, virtual assistants and speech recognition.
  • Recommendation engine: personalizes what each customer sees.
  • Sentiment analysis: measuring emotional tone and opinion in text.
  • Predictive maintenance: forecasting equipment failures before they happen.

Exam Tips

  • The cloud AI workflow is data preparation → model training → simulate and test → deployment.
  • Simulate and test is where you iterate to reduce error. Deployment makes the model available to return predictions.
  • Cloud-native management = containers, service meshes, serverless.
  • Know the use-case list: LLMs, speech AI, recommenders, fraud detection, sentiment analysis, supply chain, predictive maintenance.

Lesson 3 of 4AI in the Cloud Considerations

1. The AI Maturity Model

A framework for assessing how mature an organization's AI capabilities are. Knowing your stage guides your cloud strategy.

Stage What it looks like
1. Awareness Just exploring AI. Early conversations and experiments. No formal strategy or investment
2. Active AI experimentation Experimenting in a data science context, with a small team of data scientists and engineers. AI isn't pervasive yet
3. Operational AI in production AI in production, automating processes and improving decisions. Best practices established
4. Systemic AI AI is fully part of the digital strategy and a core component of every new digital project. A defined AI roadmap
5. Transformational AI AI is in the organization's DNA. A strong AI culture, and AI is in every part of the business

2. Starting On-Premises

  • Owning hardware doesn't mean you're mature. An on-prem organization can still be at an early stage.
  • On-prem usually means one of two decisions:
    • TCO favors ownership. The ongoing cost of reserved cloud instances was weighed against the fixed cost of a system. This is the CapEx vs. OpEx trade-off.
    • Data must stay inside your own data center.
On-prem benefit Detail
Data sovereignty and compliance You control your data and meet regulations
Fastest iteration speed Spin resources up and down quickly to speed up development cycles
Predictable costs Costs scale linearly with usage, which makes budgeting easier

3. Starting in the Cloud

  • Many organizations are cloud-first or cloud-only.
  • Elastic scale: resources grow and shrink with changing (temporal) needs.
  • Minimal barriers to entry: every cloud provider offers NVIDIA GPU instances, and you can turn them on and off like a faucet.
  • Best fit for early-stage organizations: they're still experimenting, training runs are short, models are small and datasets are limited.
  • As they mature, models and datasets grow, needs become constant, and cloud operating expenses rise. Many customers are already at this inflection point.

4. Cloud or On-Prem? The Numbers

Fact Number
Performance boost from specialized AI infrastructure vs. cloud, when data is local and compute is in the cloud 2–6×
Extra latency from distance between data and cloud +1 ms per 60 miles, which raises the cost of data gravity
Large-enterprise IT decision makers who think on-prem security is stronger than cloud 62%

The answer is hybrid

  • It's not one or the other. Use both, depending on where you are in your AI journey.
  • Start in the cloud: small experiments with cloud-hosted training, to build skills on a small budget.
  • Inflection point: as datasets grow, data gravity bites, developers spend more time grooming each training run to avoid failure, and iteration slows. That's when you may move production-scale work on-prem.

A common hybrid pattern

  • On-prem: training, customization and optimization.
    • Full control of the training environment (hardware, software, data).
    • Data sensitivity and compliance: data stays inside your own network.
    • Data gravity alignment: compute sits next to the data, which reduces latency.
  • Cloud: production inference. Cloud scale and flexibility handle varying inference loads.

5. Key Considerations Recap

  • Strategies vary: cloud-first, "own the base, rent the spike", or multi-cloud hybrid.
  • Some AI workloads must stay on-prem or in specific cloud regions because of real-time performance, data residency or data sovereignty.
Consideration Meaning
Data locality Move compute closer to the data to cut network congestion and improve performance
Data sovereignty Follow country-specific rules on where data from a location must be stored and processed
Hybrid IT strategies Hybrid and multi-cloud, using best-of-breed options for POCs, training and deployment at scale
Real-time performance Apps that must respond in real time, or give real-time analytics on sensor data

Key Terms

  • AI maturity model: 5 stages, awareness → active experimentation → operational → systemic → transformational.
  • TCO: total cost of ownership, used to compare owning systems with renting cloud instances.
  • CapEx vs. OpEx: buying hardware up front vs. paying ongoing operating costs (cloud).
  • Data gravity: large datasets pull compute toward them. Distance adds latency (about 1 ms per 60 miles) and cost.
  • Data sovereignty: country rules on where data must be stored and processed.
  • Data locality: putting compute close to the data.
  • "Own the base, rent the spike": own capacity for steady demand, and use the cloud for peaks.
  • Inflection point: where growing cloud costs and data gravity make on-prem more attractive.

Exam Tips

  • Know the five maturity stages in order: Awareness, Active Experimentation, Operational, Systemic, Transformational.
  • On-prem benefits: data sovereignty and compliance, fastest iteration, predictable costs that scale linearly.
  • Cloud benefits: elastic scale, low barrier to entry (NVIDIA GPUs at every CSP). Best for early-stage experimentation.
  • Common hybrid pattern: train on-prem, run inference in the cloud.
  • Numbers: 2–6× performance with specialized infrastructure, 1 ms of latency per 60 miles, 62% trust on-prem security more.
  • Four considerations: data locality, data sovereignty, hybrid IT, real-time performance.

Lesson 4 of 4Supported Cloud Providers and Consumption Models

1. NVIDIA Works With All Major CSPs

NVIDIA partners with the leading cloud service providers (CSPs), so you can deploy AI on the provider you choose:

  • Amazon Web Services (AWS)
  • Microsoft Azure
  • Google Cloud Platform (GCP)
  • Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI)

The ecosystem keeps growing, so you can scale AI workloads across different clouds with flexibility, reliability and performance.

2. Consumption Models: Who Manages What

  • The models differ in how responsibility is split between the customer and the CSP.
  • On-premises is the baseline. The customer manages the whole stack: data center, network, storage, physical servers, virtualization, OS, scaling, application code and data configuration.
Model The CSP manages The customer manages
On-premises Nothing Everything
IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service) On-demand infrastructure Everything above the infrastructure
PaaS (Platform as a Service) Builds on IaaS: hardware and software Their code (developers focus on coding)
SaaS (Software as a Service) The complete application and all hosting Using the app
  • First step for AI in the cloud: understand your company's current cloud strategy, including its enterprise cloud contracts and the services it already uses (e.g. IaaS or PaaS).

3. Cloud Marketplaces

A cloud marketplace is a one-stop shop for cloud services from many providers. It's one of the most popular ways to get started.

Benefit Detail
One-stop shop Compare and choose services from many providers
Easy discovery A central place to find the right services
Streamlined procurement Buy and deploy quickly, without a long procurement process
Cost savings Transparent pricing and cost comparison across providers
Increased agility Scale up or down quickly as business needs change

Key Terms

  • CSP: cloud service provider (AWS, Azure, GCP, OCI).
  • IaaS: the CSP runs the infrastructure, and the customer handles everything above it.
  • PaaS: the CSP runs the hardware and software platform, and developers just write code.
  • SaaS: the CSP delivers the complete application.
  • Cloud marketplace: a one-stop shop to discover, compare and buy cloud services.

Exam Tips

  • The four major CSPs NVIDIA supports are AWS, Azure, GCP and OCI.
  • Responsibility moves to the CSP as you go On-prem → IaaS → PaaS → SaaS.
  • PaaS = focus on code. SaaS = complete application. IaaS = infrastructure only.
  • Marketplace benefits: one-stop shop, easy discovery, streamlined procurement, cost savings, agility.
  • The first step is to understand the company's current cloud strategy and contracts.
